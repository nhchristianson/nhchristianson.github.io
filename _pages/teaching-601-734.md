## Description

Artificial intelligence and machine learning hold significant promise for improving algorithmic decision-making across domains. This course will survey recent advances in integrating AI/ML models into algorithm design. We will focus on two different paradigms: algorithms with predictions, which seek to leverage black-box, potentially unreliable predictions to improve performance while maintaining robustness; and learning-based approaches, where AI/ML models are trained to directly perform algorithmic reasoning. Throughout, we will emphasize settings where provable guarantees—such as robustness to prediction error and generalization bounds—can be obtained.

Topics will include:

- online and approximation algorithms with machine-learned predictions;
- data-driven algorithm design and selection; and
- machine learning for algorithmic reasoning.

## Course activities

- **[Lectures]({{ '/teaching/601-734/schedule/' | relative_url }})**: Lectures will cover recent papers related to the course topic, and will be focused on proving the main technical results in each paper.
- **[Readings and online discussion posts]({{ '/teaching/601-734/schedule/' | relative_url }})**: Students should read each week's paper(s) before that week's lectures to become familiar with the problem setting, motivation, and high-level main results. There is no need to read and understand the proofs in depth, as this will be the focus of lecture time. A reading-response prompt will be due on the Canvas discussion board each Monday. Students should write a 2-3 sentence response describing, e.g., their thoughts on the paper, its strengths and weaknesses, any questions about the results, or ideas for follow-up work.
- **[Problem set]({{ '/teaching/601-734/schedule/' | relative_url }})**: One problem set will be assigned in the first two weeks of class, covering foundational topics that will be useful throughout the course. 
- **[Paper presentations]({{ '/teaching/601-734/paper-presentations/' | relative_url }})**: During the last few weeks of class, students will present (in groups of 2-3) recent research papers related to the course topic.
- **[Project]({{ '/teaching/601-734/project/' | relative_url }})**: Students will complete (in groups of 1-3) a semester-long research project related to the course topics and their interests.
